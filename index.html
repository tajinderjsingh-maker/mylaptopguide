The five that matter

Learn these,
ignore the rest.

Spec sheets are built to overwhelm you. For design work, five things decide whether a laptop serves you or fights you. Understand them once and you can judge any machine.

01 — DISPLAY & COLOUR

The screen is the work

You'll trust it for years to tell the truth about colour. The red you pick must be the red that prints.

AIM FOR 100% sRGB · ideally P3 · 400+ nits
02 — MEMORY (RAM)

Room to think

How many things your machine holds at once — layers, tabs, apps. Too little and everything crawls.

AIM FOR 16GB minimum · 8GB only for light work
03 — CHIP (PERFORMANCE)

Quiet speed

Apple M-series and modern Core Ultra / Ryzen AI are all fast enough. Battery and heat matter more day-to-day.

AIM FOR Apple M-series · or Core Ultra / Ryzen AI
04 — STORAGE

Where it all lives

Design files multiply fast. 256GB fills within a year; you usually can't upgrade it later, so buy enough now.

AIM FOR 512GB SSD · 256GB is tight
05 — BATTERY & WEIGHT

The one you'll carry

If it's heavy or dies by lunch, you'll leave it home — and the best laptop is the one you have with you.

AIM FOR 12+ hrs real use · under ~1.6kg
● THE SHORTCUT

If you remember one thing

Reach the sweet spot — 16GB memory, 512GB storage, a colour-true screen — and stop. Everything else is a detail.

The Starter

Enough to begin, not to grow into

Solid for foundational 2D work. Expect to add RAM, live with an average screen, and upgrade sooner.

e.g. Acer Aspire 5 (upgrade to 16GB RAM)
₹90K–1.2L
The Sweet Spot ★

Where nearly everyone should land

Best value in the guide. Silent or colour-perfect, 16GB standard, all-day battery, a screen you can trust. If you can reach it, do.

e.g. MacBook Air M4 · Zenbook 14 OLED
₹1.6L+
The Pro

Only if the work demands it

Sustained power for 3D, motion, video; reference displays. For pure 2D, keep the money for a tablet or drive.

e.g. MacBook Pro 14 · M4
